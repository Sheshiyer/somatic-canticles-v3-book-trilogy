# T-026: Moral Premise Audit — Book 3 (Chapters 16–27)

**Premise under audit:** Coherence-through-integration vs. coherence-through-control. The four protagonists must each complete an enantiodromia — a reversal at the peak of their characteristic strength — from their signature blind spot (a control strategy) toward an integrated witness posture. The Gardener must tempt each with the perfected version of their control strategy, and each must refuse.

**Method:** Full read of Ch16, Ch17, Ch18, Ch20 (partial), Ch27; targeted grep/citation audits of Ch19, Ch21, Ch22, Ch23, Ch24, Ch25, Ch26. Read-only; no chapter files modified.

## 5. Gardener Temptation Refusal Audit

The Gardener sequence (Ch17 introduction → Ch22 perfect-world offers → Ch23 Gideon's managed world → Ch24 severance) delivers a distinct temptation per character, each tailored to the control strategy:

- **Corv — offered beautiful closure.** Refused; refusal recorded in Jian's ledger: "WITNESS MUST NOT USE SUFFERING BY MAKING IT SATISFYING" (Ch22 L709–716). Premise-verbatim. ✓
- **Sona — offered painless rest.** Refused explicitly: "This is not peace. It is a very polite burial" (Ch22 L324–338). ✓
- **Jian — offered complete explanation/certainty.** Refused; then stress-tested harder in Ch24 when the final trace is genuinely lost (L1067) — refusal survives actual deprivation, not just the offer. ✓
- **Gideon — offered managed world (Ch23) after his offer is deleted unopened (Ch22 L722–724).** Refused in premise-verbatim language: "Protection does not pre-remove their becoming" (L357–363); record line L406 states the refusal condition: "when reduced harm required reduced life." ✓

**Cross-check — does any character accept a Gardener term anywhere in Ch22–27?** No acceptance found. Ch24's severance from the maintained rule (ledger L35–41) converts all four refusals into body-costs, confirming the refusals are enacted, not merely spoken. Ch27's litany (L692–699) — "did not need his meaning… feel everything… map everything… hold everything shut… turn everything into an ending" — functions as the book's own summary of the four refusals.

**Gardener audit verdict: PASS — 4/4 temptations offered, 4/4 refused, costs paid on-page, no backsliding in Ch25–27.**

## 6. Flagged Issues + Remediation Notes

**FLAG-1 (minor): Sona's mid-book premise load is back-weighted.**
Ch16–21 give Sona functional beats (holding the Note, "Joy has to be risked" Ch20 L186) but her blind spot — absorbing/flooding — is not tested as directly as the other three's until Ch22. The arc still lands (Ch25 reflex-break + Ch27 endpoint line), so this is a density observation, not a failure.
*Remediation (optional, not applied):* one beat in Ch17–19 where Sona deliberately declines to absorb someone's distress — a small "channel not container" rehearsal — would distribute her arc more evenly. Defer to author; no edit made under read-only constraint.

**FLAG-2 (cosmetic): Gideon's offer-four handling spans two chapters.**
Ch22 deletes the offer unopened; Ch23 then stages the managed-world temptation anyway. This is coherent (deleting the offer doesn't stop the Gardener from speaking), but a reader could momentarily read Ch23 as redundant. The text already differentiates them (Ch22 = his own perfect world, refused sight-unseen; Ch23 = the Gardener's managed world, refused on principle), so no change recommended — noting only for future consistency passes.

**FLAG-3 (none):** No premise violations, no accepted temptations, no missing endpoints. All four enantiodromia lines verified verbatim in Ch27.
