# CR-01.01 critique reconciliation

Status: first-pass reconciliation after full 27-chapter inventory. This closes the sampled-critique uncertainty for CR-01.01 and opens canon-decision work for CR-01.02.

## Confirmed

- Chapter 6 is the correct first pilot. It contains all four characters, an object decision, humor that belongs to the scene, and a real flaw: retaining the shard and assigning Gideon the carrying risk are not yet distinct acts of consent.
- Chapter 14 is the correct transfer test. It already stages different readiness and receiver consent, but it needs a costly second handoff or failed transfer for the lesson to become action under pressure.
- Teamwork is strongest when one person changes behavior because of another person's lens: Jian stops Sona in Chapter 7, Sona changes Jian's scanner in Chapters 6/8, Gideon changes boundary logic in Chapter 3, Corv owns delay in Chapter 5, and the late trilogy repeatedly lets another person's silence/anger/rest remain unconverted.
- Sona's invisible labor is real in Chapters 2, 4, 6, 7, 12, 13, and 19. The text often notices it with wit or ledger language; it still needs later consequences where another person carries a task she would usually absorb.
- Gideon's protection arc is reversible and specific. Its danger is not generic control; it is the wish to preserve loved people by reducing life. Chapter 23 is the clearest expression.
- Jian's precision arc is strongest when precision protects another person's cost and weakest when it jumps toward total map, perfect status, or hidden notes.
- Corv's witness arc is strongest when he refuses beauty as closure but keeps beauty in ordinary use. Chapter 22 and Chapter 27 support this well.
- Book 2 has real structural targets: appended or repeated material in Chapters 9-11, repeated harmonization in Chapter 13, and unclear payer language in Chapter 12.
- Book 3 already protects reversibility through pauses, dry intervals, abort controls, lockouts, body-cost ledgers, deliberate release, and no retrospective claim over Mira.

## Narrowed

- The old concern that Chapter 15 risks merger is only partly true. The chapter already resists easy fusion through no-fusion terms, provisionality, field hunger, and Sona's audio file. Later revisions should sharpen privacy/interiority rather than rewrite the chapter as if it has no resistance.
- The Chapter 24 "no edges" concern must be reconciled with surrounding evidence. Chapters 24-27 repeatedly preserve distinct witnesses, handles, boundaries, openings, privacy, and ordinary vulnerability. CR-01.02 should decide which inherited command loses jurisdiction rather than treating ripening as edge-erasure.
- Chapter 13's repetition problem is not flat. The late bodily escalation partially repairs the concern; the revision target is earlier repeated successful harmonization, not the whole chapter premise.
- Chapter 16's "clean data" concern is better framed as institutional readability and concealed interpersonal cost. Mira's outcome is not presented as a simple grade of the team's enlightenment.
- Chapter 21's late duplicate/restart-like material needs audit, but the core one-second hostile-contact test is strong and should be protected.

## Rejected

- Do not treat the team as needing to outgrow dependence or become independent from each other. The ending shows interdependence becoming less possessive, not disappearing.
- Do not assign one fixed sheath or engine ownership per character. Every character carries all layers; their differences show in how a layer becomes habit, defense, temptation, and repair.
- Do not make refusal the proof of growth. Book 3 already warns that refusal can become identity; revisions should include appropriate acceptance of rest, beauty, comfort, maps, and boundaries when they remain answerable to life.
- Do not use app or Selemene engine status as canon proof. Engine outputs and app surfaces remain source/reference infrastructure until explicitly integrated by evidence.
