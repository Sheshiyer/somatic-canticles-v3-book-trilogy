## Revision candidates

### C1-A — distinguish inherited protection from deliberate authorship of the wound

At line 131, Corv says the subject built the architecture “one conscious decision at a time.” In context, this risks making survival architecture equivalent to freely chosen harm and lets the narrator settle more than the team has established. The principal's responsibility model concerns how a person responds and learns; it does not require every inherited pattern to have been knowingly authored.

Candidate replacement for that paragraph, preserving the surrounding staircase imagery:

> Vapor from each step began forming shapes—faces, hands, a child's toy, depending on where Corv stood. He watched a small wooden wheel turn once and disappear. “Something kept this moment in place,” he said. “Some of it may have been chosen. Some of it may have been the only protection available.”
>
> Jian lifted his eyes from the lattice. “Can you tell which?”
>
> Corv looked again. The wheel returned, caught in the same unfinished turn. “Not yet.”

Why this candidate: Corv makes uncertainty explicit through dialogue and changes his conduct. It creates a beginning for later discernment without explaining destiny in an opening lecture. This is a new proposed exchange, not evidence that the existing canon contains it.

Related follow-up at line 75: review the certainty of the declaration that the architecture “chose self-harm over revision.” Keep the threat and defensive function visible while deciding how much Corv actually knows. Do not make a global replacement until surrounding character and world rules are reconciled.

### C1-B — let confession begin accountability

At lines 305–311, Corv admits selling his mother's neural map for concert tickets. The answer about out-of-tune flutes can make the poor concert, rather than the betrayal, seem the reason it was wrong. The chapter does not need to resolve this history, but the admission should create an obligation the story remembers.

Candidate replacement for the exchange after his admission:

> Sona glanced sideways. “Did the concert justify the betrayal?”
>
> “No.” Corv's mouth began the shape of a joke. He let it go. “I wanted the tickets. I treated what was hers as something I could spend.”
>
> “Did you tell her?”
>
> The corridor narrowed ahead of them. Corv kept his eyes on it. “Not all of it.”
>
> Sona walked beside him for another beat. “Then you know one thing that is still yours to do.”

Why this candidate: it gives his choice, the harm, and unfinished ownership separate space. Sona's response allows growth without absolving him. It introduces a proposed fact about incomplete disclosure; verify against the wider Corv history before accepting. No reconciliation, forgiveness, or future scene is presumed.
